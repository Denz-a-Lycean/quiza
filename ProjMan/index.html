📗 Lesson 1 — Introduction to Project Management

Course & Core Definitions

Course
Project Management (OPMN03C) — provides an in-depth understanding of PM principles, techniques, and best practices: initiating, planning, executing, monitoring, controlling, and closing projects.
Project
A temporary undertaking created to produce a unique product, service, system, or result (e.g. a mobile app, university event, cybersecurity system).
Project Management
The organized process of planning, executing, monitoring, and completing a project — turns an idea into an achievable, valuable outcome.

The DNA of a project — T.U.G.R.C.

Temporary
Has a defined beginning and finish line.
Unique
Creates a particular product, service, or result.
Goal-Oriented
Drives toward accomplishing specific objectives.
Resource-Constrained
Operates with limited people, money, tech, and time.
Coordinated
Synchronizes activities, schedules, and resources.
The Illusion vs. The Reality
Illusion: a good idea alone leads straight to success. Reality: unclear requirements, unrealistic deadlines, limited resources, poor communication, unmanaged risks, and uncontrolled changes get in the way — structure prevents chaos.
Old Paradigm (Output-Driven)
Success = scope → schedule → budget → output. Now considered incomplete.
Modern Paradigm (Outcome-Driven)
Vision → Deliverable → Benefit → Value/Outcomes, in a cycle. "Projects do not simply produce outputs; they enable those outputs to drive outcomes that ultimately deliver value." — PMBOK® Guide, 7th Ed.
Project vs. Operation
Project: temporary and unique. Operation: ongoing and repetitive, maintaining an existing process.
Output vs. Outcome vs. Value
Output is what the project produces; outcome is what happens because of it; value is the benefit created.

Project Team Roles

Project Manager Responsibilities
Define objectives · Plan the project · Organize the team · Manage time · Manage resources · Manage risks · Communicate.
Project Manager
Coordinates the project and aligns people, tasks, resources, time, risk, quality, and stakeholders.
Business Analyst
Gathers, analyzes, and documents stakeholder and system requirements.
UI/UX Designer
Designs the user interface and user experience.
Programmer
Develops and maintains the system software.
Database Developer
Designs and manages the data architecture and database.
QA / Tester
Tests the system and verifies that quality requirements are met.

How projects fit into overarching organizational strategy and business objectives.

12 Principles — 4 Quadrants

Mindset & Stewardship
Be a diligent, respectful steward · Focus on value · Apply systems thinking.
People & Culture
Collaborative team environment · Engage stakeholders · Demonstrate leadership behaviors.
Navigation & Resilience
Navigate complexity · Optimize risk responses · Embrace adaptability · Enable change.
Execution & Craft
Build quality into processes · Tailor based on context.

8 Project Performance Domains

Stakeholders (engagement & alignment) · Team (culture & leadership) · Development Approach (cadence & methodology) · Planning (estimation & alignment) · Project Work (processes & resources) · Delivery (value & quality) · Measurement (tracking & troubleshooting) · Uncertainty (risk & ambiguity). Key insight: they run concurrently, not sequentially.

8 Guiding Questions

Problem (what must be addressed?) · Goal (what should it accomplish?) · Scope (what work must be completed?) · Ownership (who performs/approves?) · Timeline (when finished?) · Resources (what's required?) · Risk (what may affect it?) · Success (how measured?).

The Five Building Blocks

1 · Objectives
SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.
2 · Scope
The included work bounded against excluded work. Beware scope creep — uncontrolled addition of requirements beyond agreed boundaries.
3 · Deliverables
Measurable, tangible outputs (proposal, requirements doc, system design, prototype, tested app, user manual, final report).
4 · Stakeholders
Client, sponsor, project manager, development team, users, administrators.
5 · Constraints
Scope, time, cost, quality, resources, risk — all interconnected. Changing one affects the others.

Mechanics, Life Cycle & Roles

Mechanics of PM
Start (what needs to be accomplished?) → Plan (how will we accomplish it?) → Execute (perform planned activities) → Monitor (are we following the plan?) → Complete (did we deliver what was required?).

The Project Life Cycle — 5 phases

1 · Initiation
Identify core purpose, initial objectives, key stakeholders; determine feasibility.
2 · Planning
Establish scope, schedule, budget, roles, quality standards, risk responses.
3 · Execution
Carry out planned activities, coordinate the team, produce deliverables.
4 · Monitoring & Control
Measure progress vs. plan, evaluate performance, manage risk, control changes.
5 · Closing
Obtain formal acceptance, transfer output, document lessons learned, officially complete.

7 Major Responsibilities of a PM

Define objectives · Plan the project · Organize the team · Manage time · Manage resources · Manage risks · Communicate (the critical link between all stakeholders).
The Project Manager
"The conductor, not the soloist" — doesn't perform every task, but ensures the team works together toward a shared goal: defining objectives, organizing tasks, assigning responsibilities, managing time/budget, communicating, monitoring risk, maintaining quality.

Six Golden Rules of PM

Embrace the Temporary · Balance the Scale (scope/time/cost/quality/resources/risk are forever linked) · Involve Stakeholders continuously · Anticipate Risk · Control Change (avoid scope creep) · Communicate Constantly.
📘 Lesson 2 — Project Management & the IT Context

A Systems View of Project Management

System
A group of interconnected components that work together to achieve a common goal.
Systems View of PM
Examines a project as part of the larger organizational environment — people, processes, technology, business objectives.
Systems Philosophy
Views an organization and its projects as interconnected parts rather than separate activities.
Systems Analysis
Identifying and evaluating a system's components, problems, requirements, relationships, and solutions.
Systems Management
Managing business, organizational, and technological factors so a system achieves its purpose.

Three-Sphere Model

Business
How the project supports org goals & delivers value — vision, strategy, market needs.
Organizational
People, culture, structure, leadership, policies, work processes.
Technology
Hardware, software, data, networks, infrastructure required.

Organizations & Structures

Organization
A structured group of people who work together using established roles, processes, and resources.
Organizational Structure
The arrangement of authority, responsibilities, communication, and reporting relationships.
Functional
Employees grouped by specialization/department.
Projectized
Employees assigned to projects; PM has substantial authority.
Matrix
Employees report to both a functional manager and a PM.
Weak Matrix
Functional manager has more authority than the PM.
Balanced Matrix
Authority shared between functional manager and PM.
Strong Matrix
PM has greater authority and control over resources.
Organizational Culture
Shared values, beliefs, attitudes, expectations, and behaviors that influence how people work.
Organizational Change
A modification in technology, structure, processes, policies, or employee responsibilities.
Resistance to Change
Unwillingness/hesitation to accept a new system, procedure, or arrangement.
Project Governance
The framework of policies, responsibilities, authority, and decision-making used to direct/control a project.

Stakeholder Management

Stakeholder
Any person, group, or organization that can affect, be affected by, or perceive itself to be affected by a project.
Internal
Employee, manager, project team member.
External
Customer, vendor, consultant, government agency.
Stakeholder Register
Document with names, roles, interests, influence, expectations, communication requirements.
Stakeholder Analysis
Examining stakeholders by power, interest, influence, expectations, potential impact.
Power–Interest Grid
Classifies stakeholders by level of authority and interest.
Stakeholder Engagement Plan
Explains how stakeholders will be informed, consulted, involved, managed.
Stakeholder Management Process
Identify stakeholders → Analyze their power, interest, and impact → Engage and Communicate with them → Monitor changes in their needs and influence.

Power–Interest strategies

Project Sponsor
Provides resources, organizational support, and high-level direction.
Project Team
Performs the work needed to achieve project objectives.
End User
Directly uses the final product, service, or system.

Context of IT Projects

IT Project
A temporary effort to create, improve, implement, or maintain a technology-based product, service, or system.
Scope Creep
Uncontrolled addition of requirements without proper approval or adjustment.
Project Constraint
A limiting factor: scope, time, cost, quality, resources, risk.
Project Risk
An uncertain event/condition that may positively or negatively affect objectives.
System Integration
Connecting different apps, databases, devices, platforms so they work together.
User Acceptance
Willingness of intended users to approve, adopt, and properly use a new system.
Cybersecurity
Protecting systems/networks/apps/data from unauthorized access or attack.
Data Privacy
Proper collection, processing, storage, use, protection of personal/sensitive info.
Technical Feasibility
Whether available tech, skills, infrastructure can support the project.
Operational Feasibility
Whether the system can function within existing operations.
DevOps
Integrates dev & IT operations to deliver systems faster and more reliably.
DevSecOps
Integrates security practices throughout dev and operations.
Cloud Computing
Delivery of computing resources over the internet.
Artificial Intelligence
Enables systems to perform tasks needing human intelligence — learning, reasoning, decisions.
Innovation
Creating or implementing new ideas, approaches, technologies, or solutions within an organization or project.
